# Expanded catalog and search

The catalog has 96 entries, including 16 additional engineering fields and statistics, actuarial science, geology, and food science. Curriculum and job titles are illustrative; degree names, concentrations, and professional requirements vary by institution and location. The new entries intentionally show “Varies by role” instead of inventing a single median wage for a major.

Search covers names, aliases, categories, careers, courses, skills, school subjects, AP courses, and directions. Exact names and aliases rank highest. Each query word must match; minor spelling errors in long title words are accepted. An active category remains visible and is respected; the empty state can search all categories without clearing the query. Search relevance takes priority over personalized ranking while a query is present.

Every catalog entry has a matching profile. The model version advances to interests-v5, retaining the same 24 answer IDs and values. Saved answers are rescored for display without rewriting historical assessments.

Connected directions and related-major links open real catalog pages. Concentrations without standalone pages are identified as focus areas rather than disabled buttons. Detail sections use compact neutral pill tags; direction links use concise rows without descriptions.


## Fashion, luxury, and quantitative expansion

Twenty additional pages cover luxury management, fashion design and management, textiles, accessories, jewelry, footwear, fashion communication, industrial design, quantitative finance, financial engineering, econometrics, quantitative economics, operations research, applied mathematics, business analytics, risk management, fintech, and retail management. Each has curriculum examples, skills, career directions, related pages, searchable aliases, and an assessment profile. Field-specific focus areas are retained alongside navigable specialization links.

These are exploration fields, not a claim that every title is an undergraduate major. Luxury and financial engineering pathways in particular can be concentrations or graduate programs. Career labels describe possible directions, not guaranteed entry-level qualifications. Salary remains ?Varies by role?.
